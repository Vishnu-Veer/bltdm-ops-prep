    The game plan for any case

    Five moves, in this order. Run them every time and you'll hit the PIs without thinking about it.

    1. Name the problem in one sentence

      "Your cheapest supplier keeps shipping bad product, and that puts your customers and your reputation at risk."

    2. Explain the idea

      Most PIs start with explain, describe or discuss. Define the concept like you're teaching the owner: what a supply chain is, what quality control means, why safety rules exist.

    3. Bring the law

      Name real things: OSHA for workplace safety, contracts with vendors, product liability, data privacy, the FTC. This is where Business Law and Ethics teams win points.

    4. Bring the ethics

      Is it fair to workers, customers and suppliers? Legal isn't always ethical. Mention a code of ethics, being honest with customers and not cutting corners to save money.

    5. Give the plan and how you'll measure it

      Who does what, by when, and what it costs. Then the numbers that prove it worked: defect rate, injury reports, on-time delivery, customer complaints.

    Words that make judges nod

    OSHA
    The federal agency for workplace safety. Injuries get reported, hazards get fixed.
    Product liability
    A company can be sued if what it sells hurts someone.
    Breach of contract
    When a vendor or the business doesn't do what the contract says.
    Due diligence
    Checking a vendor out before you sign, not after something goes wrong.
    Corrective action plan
    A written fix with a deadline you give a vendor or team.
    Total cost of ownership
    The real cost of a choice: price plus returns, downtime and risk.
    Just-in-time inventory
    Ordering stock right when you need it, which saves money but breaks if suppliers are late.
    KPI
    The number you track to prove the plan is working.
